Research Vision

Understanding how sleep protects the brain from neurodegeneration requires connecting biological mechanisms across multiple scales—from molecular regulation and cellular physiology to neuroimaging and computational analysis.

My research aims to identify the mechanisms that regulate the brain's glymphatic system, with a particular focus on the water channel Aquaporin-4 (AQP4). By combining quantitative microscopy, animal models, neuroimaging, genetics and artificial intelligence, I seek to understand how disturbances in AQP4 regulation contribute to impaired brain clearance and ultimately to neurodegenerative disease.

Although my current work focuses on the glymphatic system, it builds upon nearly two decades of experience in quantitative neuroimaging methodology and PET imaging. The common theme throughout my research has been the development and application of quantitative methods to understand brain physiology.
